Getting Started

1. Add the component

Built-In Render Pipeline: Add the ColoredPencilPostProcess component directly to your Camera.

Universal Render Pipeline (URP): Open your active Universal Renderer Data asset, click Add Renderer Feature and choose Colored Pencil Post Process Feature.

You can also open one of the sample scenes in Samples/. For the URP sample, assign Samples/Resources/URP/URP 17 Colored Pencil Renderer in Project Settings > Graphics, or add the feature to your own renderer.

2. Start from a preset

In the Preset Manager at the top of the Inspector, drag one of the presets from Samples/Presets into Active Preset and click Load:

3. Tune the look

Every field has a small reset button next to it to instantly restore its default value.

  • Hatching - Anchor Hatching To Scene keeps the strokes on the surfaces when the camera moves (recommended). Stroke Length and Stroke Width shape the pencil lines (pixels at 1080p; they scale with the resolution). Pressure covers more paper in every tone, Softness goes from crisp to smudged lines. Cross Hatching and Cross Hatch Angle add a second layer in dark areas.
  • Stroke Flow - Flow Smoothness makes the hatching follow large shapes instead of small details. Flat Area Threshold and Flat Area Stroke Angle control the hatching of flat areas such as skies.
  • Color - Color Fill lays a light flat color under the strokes. Color Pressure works bright but colorful areas (a green meadow) with pencil instead of leaving them as paper. Saturation and Brightness complete the group.
  • Paper - Paper Color, and Paper Grain / Paper Grain Scale for the tooth of the paper the pencil skips over.
  • Outline - Outline Strength, Outline Width, Outline Detail (how many edges become lines), Texture Suppression and Outline Color.

Tip: if dark scribbles appear inside textured surfaces, raise Texture Suppression or lower Outline Detail. If object contours are missing, do the opposite.

4. Save your look as a Preset

Once you're happy with a set of values, you can save them for reuse:

  1. In the Preset Manager section at the top of the Inspector, click Create New Preset Asset.
  2. Give it a name, this creates a ColoredPencilPreset asset in your project.
  3. Use Save to store your current settings into the preset, or Load to apply a preset's settings to the current component.

This is the easiest way to share a consistent look across multiple cameras, scenes and render pipelines.

5. Motion: anchored hatching & Temporal Stability

Screen-space sketch filters usually suffer from the "shower door" effect: the strokes stay still while the world moves behind them. With Anchor Hatching To Scene the hatching is generated on the 3D surfaces (and on the sky, as directions), so it travels with them during pans, orbits and camera moves. The paper grain stays on the screen, like a real sheet of paper.

Temporal Stability (default 0.8) blends the current frame with the previous one, reprojected with the depth buffer and the camera motion. History that no longer matches the current frame is rejected (variance clipping), so moving objects don't leave trails, and the blend is reduced during fast motion to keep the image sharp.

  • Both features need the camera depth texture. The component and the renderer feature request it automatically.
  • Set Temporal Stability to 0 for static images.

Tip: the filter can only be as stable as your scene. For the cleanest result, enable anti-aliasing (MSAA) and soft shadows, and use alpha clipping (cutout) instead of transparency for foliage.

6. Performance tuning

The cost is mostly proportional to the stroke length and the number of hatching layers. Measured on a GeForce GTX 1650 at 1920x1080: about 10 ms for the default presets and 7 ms for Mobile.

  • Stroke Length - the biggest lever: shorter strokes are much cheaper.
  • Cross Hatching - set it to 0 to skip the second hatching layer.
  • Flow Field Scale - resolution used to detect the hatching direction (Half by default). Quarter is cheaper with a small loss of precision.

7. Capture a screenshot

The Utility Tools section lets you capture a high-resolution screenshot of the stylized result directly, at up to 4x your Game View resolution - handy for portfolio shots or marketing material.

Notes & limitations

  • By default the effect is injected Before Rendering Post Processing, so bloom, tonemapping and color grading are applied on top of the drawing. Use the Injection Point of the renderer feature to change this.
  • Anchored hatching needs a perspective camera and a depth texture. Orthographic cameras and 2D scenes use screen-space hatching, and Temporal Stability assumes a static view.
  • Transparent objects that don't write depth (particles, glass) are hatched like the surface behind them.
  • Strokes keep their size on screen, so the pattern on a surface changes gradually as the camera gets closer or farther.
