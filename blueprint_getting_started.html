Getting Started

1. Add the component

Built-In Render Pipeline: Add the BlueprintPostProcess component directly to your Camera.

Universal Render Pipeline (URP): Open your active Universal Renderer Data asset, click Add Renderer Feature and choose Blueprint Post Process Feature.

You can also open one of the sample scenes in Samples/: a 3D valley with dimensions and a photo, for each pipeline. For the URP samples, assign Samples/Resources/URP/URP 17 Blueprint Renderer in Project Settings > Graphics, or add the feature to your own renderer.

2. Start from a preset

In the Preset Manager at the top of the Inspector, drag one of the presets from Samples/Presets into Active Preset and click Load:

Preset Look
Cyanotype Classic blueprint: white lines and grid on deep blue paper, with a title block.
Drafting Blue ink on white drafting paper, light volume fill and hatched shadows.
Neon CAD Glowing cyan lines on black, with a grid on the ground only.
Pencil Draft Graphite on cream graph paper, wobbly hand-drawn lines, measurements in feet and inches.
AR Scanner Glowing lines and grid over the original colors of the scene.
Mobile Cyanotype with thinner lines, geometry lines only (skips the image-line passes), a ground grid and no paper grain.

3. Tune the look

Every field has a small reset button next to it to instantly restore its default value. Sizes are in pixels at 1080p and scale with the resolution.

  • Lines - Edge Source chooses where lines come from: Auto (geometry where the scene writes depth, image colors elsewhere), Geometry, Image or both. Outline Width is for silhouettes, Crease Width and Crease Angle for interior edges, Depth Sensitivity for how small a depth jump becomes a line. Image Line Width, Image Sensitivity and Image Texture Suppression control the lines drawn from colors.
  • Line Style - Line Glow and Glow Radius for a CRT or hologram glow, Line Wobble and Wobble Scale for hand-drawn lines, Line Fade Distance to fade far lines (meters).
  • Grid - Grid Mode: Surfaces (every surface), Ground (horizontal surfaces only), Screen (printed on the paper) or Off. Grid Size in meters (or Screen Grid Size in pixels), Major Line Every, colors, opacities, width and Grid Fade Distance.
  • Shading - Fill Amount and Fill Color keep volumes readable. Scene Color Mix shows the original colors (1 = scanner / AR look). Fill Background also applies them to the sky and sprites. Shadow Hatching, Shadow Threshold, Hatch Spacing and Hatch Angle hatch the dark areas.
  • Paper - Paper Color, Paper Edge Color and Vignette for the sheet, Paper Grain and Paper Grain Scale for its texture.

4. Add dimensions

Measurements are chosen per object with the BlueprintDimension component. Objects without it show no measurements.

  1. Select one or more objects and click Add Blueprint Dimension to Selected Objects in the Annotations section of the filter, or use Add Component > Blueprint Dimension.
  2. Choose what to measure in Measure:
    • Height, Width or Depth - the size of the bounding box of the object and its children along that axis.
    • Between Points - the distance between two Transforms (Point A and Point B), for example two doors.
  3. Adjust Offset and Offset Direction to move the dimension line away from the object. Show Label, Prefix (for example "H = ") and Label Override (a fixed text) control the label, and Override Color gives the dimension its own color.

The selected dimension is drawn as a gizmo in the Scene view. Global options are in the Annotations section of the filter:

  • Show Dimensions - shows or hides all dimensions at once.
  • Units - meters, centimeters, millimeters, feet, feet and inches, or scene units. The scene is assumed to be in meters.
  • Decimals, Terminator (tick, arrow or dot), Text Size and Dimension Line Width.
  • Dash Hidden Dimensions - draws the part of a dimension behind geometry as a dashed line, like a hidden edge.
  • Show Title Block - a drawing frame with Title, Title Block Left (for example the scale) and Title Block Right (for example the sheet number), in the corner chosen in Title Block Corner.

5. Save your look as a Preset

  1. In the Preset Manager section at the top of the Inspector, click Create New Preset Asset.
  2. Give it a name, this creates a BlueprintPreset asset in your project.
  3. Use Save to store your current settings into the preset, or Load to apply a preset's settings to the current component.

6. Motion & Temporal Stability

The grid, the hatching and the wobble are generated on the 3D surfaces rebuilt from the depth buffer, so they move with the world during pans, orbits and camera moves. Their spacing stays constant on screen: as you get closer, finer levels blend in smoothly instead of popping.

Temporal Stability (default 0.9) blends the current frame with the previous one, reprojected with the depth buffer and the camera motion. History that no longer matches the current frame is rejected (variance clipping), so moving objects don't leave trails, and the blend is reduced during fast motion to keep the image sharp. Set it to 0 for static images.

Tip: the filter can only be as stable as your scene. For the cleanest result, enable anti-aliasing (MSAA) and soft shadows, and use alpha clipping (cutout) instead of transparency for foliage.

7. Performance tuning

Measured on a GeForce GTX 1650 at 1920x1080 in the 3D sample: about 6 to 7 ms for the default presets and 2.6 ms for Mobile, which skips the image lines.

  • Line widths and glow - the distance field searches as far as the widest line plus the glow radius: thinner lines and no glow are cheaper.
  • Image lines - the biggest lever (about 3.5 ms). With Edge Source: Auto they are computed for the sky and sprites; set Edge Source to Geometry (or Image Line Width to 0) in 3D scenes to skip them.
  • Temporal Stability - set it to 0 to skip the history pass.

Notes & limitations

  • By default the effect is injected Before Rendering Post Processing, so bloom, tonemapping and color grading are applied on top of the drawing. Use the Injection Point of the renderer feature to change this.
  • Geometry lines, the grid on surfaces and Temporal Stability need the camera depth texture; the component and the renderer feature request it automatically. Perspective and orthographic cameras are supported.
  • Transparent objects that don't write depth (particles, glass) get no geometry lines; with Edge Source: Auto they are drawn from the image colors.
  • Image lines are made for photos and 2D art; on very busy textures (dense foliage) raise Image Texture Suppression.
  • Dimensions measure the axis-aligned bounding box of the renderers of an object; use Between Points for exact measurements on rotated objects.
