Getting Started

1. Add the component

Built-In Render Pipeline: Add the MangaPostProcess component directly to your Camera.

Universal Render Pipeline (URP): Open your active Universal Renderer Data asset, click Add Renderer Feature and choose Manga Post Process Feature.

You can also open one of the sample scenes in Samples/. For the URP sample, assign Samples/Resources/URP/URP 17 Manga Renderer in Project Settings > Graphics, or add the feature to your own renderer.

2. Start from a preset

In the Preset Manager at the top of the Inspector, drag one of the presets from Samples/Presets into Active Preset and click Load:

3. Tune the look

Settings are grouped in the same order as the stages of the filter. Every field has a small reset button next to it to instantly restore its default value.

  • Edge Flow - Flow Smoothness makes lines follow large shapes instead of small details.
  • Abstraction - Abstraction Passes repeats the smoothing (0 disables it). Smoothing Size and Color Tolerance control how much texture is removed before lines and tones are drawn.
  • Ink Lines - Line Width sets the thickness. Line Sensitivity and Line Threshold control how many edges become lines: higher values give more and fuller lines. Line Cleanup removes specks for clean contours. Line Coherence joins broken segments into long strokes and Line Crispness goes from soft pencil to crisp ink. Ink Fill fills shadows with solid black. Line Opacity and Ink Color complete the group.
  • Screentone & Paper - Tone Style (Dots, Lines, Crosshatch or Mixed By Color), Tone Levels, Tone Size, Tone Strength (0 = line art only), Tone Angle and Paper Color.

Tip: if lines appear in soft areas such as clouds or fog, lower Line Threshold or raise Line Cleanup. If contours are missing, do the opposite.

4. Save your look as a Preset

Once you're happy with a set of values, you can save them for reuse:

  1. In the Preset Manager section at the top of the Inspector, click Create New Preset Asset.
  2. Give it a name, this creates a MangaPreset asset in your project.
  3. Use Save to store your current settings into the preset, or Load to apply a preset's settings to the current component.

This is the easiest way to share a consistent manga look across multiple cameras, scenes and render pipelines.

5. Temporal Stability

Line detection reacts to every small change between frames, so without help lines and tones can "boil" when the camera moves. Temporal Stability (default 0.8) blends the current frame with the previous one, reprojected with the depth buffer and the camera motion. History that no longer matches the current frame is rejected (variance clipping), so moving objects don't leave trails.

  • Higher values are steadier but slightly softer during very fast motion. Set it to 0 to disable it, for example on static images.
  • It needs the camera depth texture. The component and the renderer feature request it automatically.

Tip: the filter amplifies any flicker that already exists in your scene. For the cleanest result, enable anti-aliasing (MSAA) and soft shadows, and use alpha clipping (cutout) instead of transparency for foliage.

6. Performance tuning

If you need more headroom, look at the Performance section:

  • Stylization Scale - runs the abstraction stage at half or quarter resolution. This is the biggest performance lever. Lines and screentones are always drawn at full resolution, so they stay crisp.
  • Flow Field Scale - lowers the resolution used to detect edge directions. Cheaper, with a small loss of line coherence.
  • Abstraction Passes - each pass costs two full-screen filters. Use fewer passes on low-end hardware.

7. Capture a screenshot

The Utility Tools section lets you capture a high-resolution screenshot of the stylized result directly, at up to 4x your Game View resolution - handy for portfolio shots or marketing material.

Notes & limitations

  • By default the effect is injected Before Rendering Post Processing, so bloom, tonemapping and color grading are applied on top of the manga image. Use the Injection Point of the renderer feature to change this.
  • Screentones are generated in screen space, so in motion the pattern stays aligned to the screen rather than to objects, as in animated manga.
  • Orthographic cameras assume a static view for Temporal Stability.
